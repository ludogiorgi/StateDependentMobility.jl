\section*{Periodic soft-spin stochastic Landau--Lifshitz example}

We consider a periodic one-dimensional chain of soft spins
\[
  m_i(t)\in\R^3,\qquad i=1,\ldots,N,\qquad m_{i+N}=m_i,
\]
with total state dimension \(d_x=3N\).  In the numerical experiment below we use
\(N=12\), hence \(d_x=36\).  The target invariant density is
\[
  p_{\rm ss}(m)=Z^{-1}\exp[-U(m)/\Theta],
\]
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-potential}
  U(m)=\sum_{i=1}^N\left\{
  \frac{\lambda}{4}\left(\|m_i\|^2-m_\ast^2\right)^2
  +\frac{J}{2}\|m_{i+1}-m_i\|^2
  -\frac{K}{2}(m_i\cdot e_z)^2
  \right\},
  \qquad e_z=(0,0,1)^\T .
\end{equation}
Thus the stationary score is
\[
  s_i(m):=\nabla_{m_i}\log p_{\rm ss}(m)=\Theta^{-1}H_i(m),
\]
with effective field
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:effective-field}
  H_i(m)=-\nabla_{m_i}U(m)
  =-\lambda\left(\|m_i\|^2-m_\ast^2\right)m_i
  +J(m_{i-1}+m_{i+1}-2m_i)
  +K(m_i\cdot e_z)e_z .
\end{equation}

For \(a\in\R^3\), let \([a]_\times\) denote the skew matrix satisfying
\([a]_\times v=a\times v\):
\[
  [a]_\times=\begin{pmatrix}
  0 & -a_3 & a_2\\
  a_3 & 0 & -a_1\\
  -a_2 & a_1 & 0
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
The mobility is block diagonal and translation equivariant,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-mobility}
  M_{ij}(m)=\delta_{ij}\{D_i(m)+R_i(m)\},
  \qquad
  R_i(m)=-\gamma\Theta [m_i]_\times,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-diffusion}
  D_i(m)=\Theta A_i(m),\qquad
  A_i(m)=\eps\Id_3
  +\alpha_\perp\left(\|m_i\|^2\Id_3-m_i m_i^\T\right)
  +\alpha_\parallel m_i m_i^\T .
\end{equation}
The symmetric part \(D_i\) is positive definite for \(\eps>0\), while \(R_i^\T=-R_i\).
The corresponding It\^o SDE in score-based Langevin form is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:score-based-spin-sde}
  \dd m_i
  =\left[ M_{ii}(m)s_i(m)+\nabla_{m_i}\cdot M_{ii}(m)\right]\dd t
  +\sqrt{2D_i(m)}\,\dd W_i(t),
  \qquad i=1,\ldots,N,
\end{equation}
where \(W_i\) are independent standard Brownian motions in \(\R^3\).  Equivalently,
using \(\nabla_{m_i}\cdot R_i=0\) and
\(\nabla_{m_i}\cdot A_i=(4\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp)m_i\), this is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:component-spin-sde}
  \dd m_i
  =\left\{
  A_i(m)H_i(m)-\gamma\,m_i\times H_i(m)
  +\Theta(4\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp)m_i
  \right\}\dd t
  +\sqrt{2\Theta}\,A_i(m)^{1/2}\dd W_i(t).
\end{equation}
The term \(A_iH_i\) is the dissipative gradient component, while
\(-\gamma m_i\times H_i\) is the conservative precessional component.

\paragraph{Parameter values.}
All parameters used in the proposed experiment are fixed as follows:
\[
\begin{array}{ccl@{\qquad}ccl}
N &=& 12,                  & d_x &=& 36,\\
\lambda &=& 10.0,          & m_\ast &=& 1.0,\\
J &=& 0.35,                & K &=& 0.50,\\
\Theta &=& 0.20,           & \gamma &=& 4.0,\\
\alpha_\perp &=& 0.25,     & \alpha_\parallel &=& 0.03,\\
\eps &=& 10^{-2},          & h_{\rm ext} &=& 0.
\end{array}
\]
For these values the two symmetry-related ordered states are near
\(m_i=\pm \meq e_z\), with
\[
  \meq=\left(m_\ast^2+\frac{K}{\lambda}\right)^{1/2}
  =\sqrt{1.05}\approx 1.0247 .
\]
The absence of an external field gives the spin-inversion symmetry
\(m\mapsto -m\).  Hence \(\langle R_i\rangle_{\rm ss}=0\), although
\(R_i(m)\) is order one on the support of \(p_{\rm ss}\).  This makes the
state-dependent correction \(\delta M(m)=M(m)-\langle M\rangle_{\rm ss}\)
especially important for the oscillatory finite-lag correlations.

Linearizing the transverse modes around either ordered branch gives, for
Fourier mode \(k\), the approximate damped-oscillator form
\[
  \dot u_k=-\Gamma_k u_k+\sigma\Omega_k J_2u_k,
  \qquad \sigma\in\{+1,-1\},
\]
where
\[
  \Lambda_k=K+4J\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi k}{N}\right),\qquad
  \Omega_k=\gamma \meq \Lambda_k,
\]
and
\[
  \Gamma_k=\left(\eps+\alpha_\perp\meq^2\right)\Lambda_k
  -\Theta(4\alpha_\parallel-2\alpha_\perp).
\]
Thus transverse spin correlations have the expected form
\(C_k(t)\approx \exp(-\Gamma_k t)\cos(\Omega_k t)\).

\paragraph{Suggested integration settings.}
For Euler--Maruyama data generation, use time step
\[
  \Delta t=2.5\times 10^{-4},
\]
save every
\[
  \Delta t_{\rm save}=10^{-2},
\]
and use training lags
\[
  \mathcal T=\{0.02,0.04,0.06,\ldots,1.00\}.
\]
